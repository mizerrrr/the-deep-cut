How The Deep Cut works.

The Deep Cut is a free website for finding new music without algorithms. Every search shows six random albums from the Internet Archive, each in a different style, with a player built in.

Updated 29 October 2026

The Internet Archive holds hundreds of thousands of freely licensed and historical music releases, and most of them are almost impossible to stumble upon. There is no front page that surfaces them and no chart that ranks them. The Deep Cut is a small window into that collection: press Search more and six albums arrive at random, shown as an editorial page with the cover, the tracklist and a player.

There is no feed, no account and no recommendation engine. Nothing you listen to changes what you see next. That is the point: the next album you love is probably one nobody would have recommended to you.

Frequently asked questions

Where do the albums come from?
From the Internet Archive's public music collections: netlabels, Jamendo, the Free Music Archive, Unlocked Recordings, digitized LPs, open source releases, hip-hop mixtapes, and Brazilian, Latin American and Colombian música campesina. Classical recordings are left out. The site reads them through the Archive's public search and metadata APIs.
Is it really random?
Yes. Each search samples a different slice of the archive and shuffles it, so nothing is ranked by plays or by what you listened to before. The one exception is hip-hop mixtapes, which need at least 1,000 downloads to keep out anonymous DJ compilations.
What gets filtered out?
Every pick is checked before it reaches you. An item needs at least four distinct tracks (an MP3 and a FLAC of the same song count once), an original cover image and evidence of being a real release. Podcasts, radio shows, sermons, lectures and audiobooks are dropped.
Does it filter AI-generated music?
For releases from 2025 on, yes. Albums that declare AI tools such as Suno, Udio or Stable Audio in their title, description, tags or MP3 metadata are left out, and so are albums whose cover keeps an AI image generator's default file name. It can only catch what the upload declares, so it is a strong filter, not a guarantee.
Can I choose an era?
Yes. Filters go from the 1960s through the 2000s and 2010s to a separate 2025–26 filter for new releases.
Is the music free and legal to listen to?
The music is streamed straight from the Internet Archive, which hosts it. Much of it was released by the artists themselves under Creative Commons licenses, but rights vary from item to item. Every album links back to its archive.org page, where its license or rights statement is shown. The music belongs to its artists; The Deep Cut only points to it.
Why is the artist or year sometimes missing?
The site never makes up metadata. Artist, year and region appear only when the Internet Archive record states them.
Do I need an account? What data is kept?
No account. Saved albums, liked tracks and listening history are stored only in your browser. Visits are counted with Vercel Web Analytics, which sets no cookies.
Can I install it as an app?
Yes. It installs as a web app on iPhone and iPad (Safari → Share → Add to Home Screen), Android, Windows, Mac and Linux (Chrome or Edge), and shows the track and cover on the lock screen and media keys. It can also send the music to a TV through Chromecast or AirPlay.
Who made it?
An independent developer in Brazil. The code is open source under the MIT License at github.com/mizerrrr/the-deep-cut. The Deep Cut is not affiliated with the Internet Archive. Contact: ethedeepcut@gmail.com.
