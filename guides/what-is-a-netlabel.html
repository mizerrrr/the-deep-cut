What is a netlabel?

A netlabel is a record label that lives online. It curates and releases music like any label, but gives the music away as free downloads, often under a Creative Commons license.

Published 29 October 2026 · 4 min read

A label without a warehouse

A traditional label presses records, ships them to stores and earns money on sales. A netlabel skips all of that. It chooses artists, sequences releases, commissions or designs cover art and publishes everything as files anyone can download. What it keeps from the label world is the part that matters most to listeners: curation. A netlabel has a sound and a point of view.

Most netlabels are run by one person or a small group, as a labor of love. Many release music under Creative Commons licenses, so the artists keep their rights while letting listeners share the music freely.

Where netlabels came from

Netlabels grew up with the MP3 and the early web, in the late 1990s and 2000s. Many came out of the tracker and demoscene communities, where making music on computers and sharing it for free was already the norm. As broadband spread, hundreds of labels appeared, especially in electronic, ambient, experimental, chiptune, lo-fi and dub techno.

Many of those early labels have since closed and their websites are gone. Their music often survives thanks to the Internet Archive, whose Netlabels collection preserves thousands of releases.

Why netlabels are great for discovery

  • Curated catalogs. If you like one release, the rest of the label's catalog is a strong lead.
  • No gatekeeping by sales. Music didn't have to be commercially safe to be released, so you find genuinely unusual records.
  • Free and shareable. You can stream, download and often share the music legally. Check each release's license first.
  • Mostly invisible to algorithms. Many netlabel releases were never on streaming services, so recommendation engines rarely reach them.

How to find netlabel releases

  1. Browse the Netlabels collection on archive.org: search mediatype:audio AND collection:netlabels.
  2. Follow the label, not just the artist. Each item page usually names the label; open its other releases.
  3. Look for labels still active on Bandcamp and their own sites, and support them.
  4. Let chance do it. The Deep Cut includes the Netlabels collection in its random picks, alongside Jamendo, the Free Music Archive and more.

Free to download doesn't always mean free to reuse. Before using a netlabel track in a video or a podcast, read the license on the release page and credit the artist. More in our guide to free, legal music on the Internet Archive.
